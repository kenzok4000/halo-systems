          How it works

          Each drone runs a 50 Hz controller: it tracks its slot in the formation, and a safety filter (control barrier functions) keeps it clear of other drones, obstacles and the ground. No language model is in this loop.

          Every 5 seconds a supervisor reads a short status report and may change the formation, spacing, speed, altitude or safety margin. The supervisor is either hand-written rules or NVIDIA Nemotron on Nebius Token Factory. Every directive passes a safety validator first. If Nemotron fails or replies badly, the rules take over for that step.

          The simulation includes wind and gust fronts, noisy GPS, lossy and delayed radio, motor failures, batteries and obstacles. It is a simulation, not flight hardware.

          In the wildfire valley the terrain, forest, fire spread (Rothermel's model on the land cover, slope and wind), smoke plume heights, the drones' smoke sightings, the ground station's fire locations, forecasts and alerts, and the phones they go to are all simulated; the alerts reach simulated phones only, and each waits for a person to approve it. Elsewhere the scenery, flames and smoke are drawn for looks: the simulator models those hotspots as fixed points.
