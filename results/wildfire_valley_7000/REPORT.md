## Which way the head runs

Each brief tells the fire crews which way a fire's head will run in the next hour, with a cone (a half-width either side of the bearing: the forecast ensemble's spread, widened for errors it does not represent, set on development seeds to hold the true head about 80% of the time), and why (the wind the swarm measured, the slope, the fuel ahead). The head is where the front advances furthest beyond the ground burning now, in the next hour; its bearing runs from the nearest of that ground. The true head is found the same way on the fire's true spread over the ground it reaches first, from its ignition point if it has not taken off yet. Scored: every brief in a flight's last forecast, matched to the true fire nearest it (within 60 m), whose true head advances at least 20 m in the hour (two briefs of one fire are both scored); the brief's bearing and two simple rules, straight downwind of the measured wind and straight upslope under the fire (where the slope is 2% or more), each against the true bearing (0 is exact, 180 the opposite way). A brief that said the head would not advance, though it did, counts as 180 degrees off, not within 22.5, not closer than wind-only, with a speed of 0; the cone column counts only briefs that gave a cone. Closer: the brief's error is smaller than the wind-only rule's for that fire. Not located: moving fires with no located fire within 60 m. Crew updates: briefs relayed to the crews' simulated devices when a fire is first located; again when it starts or stops spreading or advancing, its direction becomes clear or uncertain, its head turns (while its direction is clear) by 30 degrees or more and beyond the cone the crews were given, its head speed rises by half or falls by a third (and by at least 0.5 m/min), or it will reach a named place it was not going to, or reach one 15 minutes sooner by the clock; and a withdrawal when a fire is merged into another.

## Warnings to phones

Each flight has 63 simulated phones (homes, a campground, hikers, drivers and a ranch); 5% have no signal. Alerts go to whole evacuation zones at Japan-style levels (3 prepare, 4 evacuate, 5 emergency), automatically by fixed rules once each passes the alert check, and arrive 5 to 30 s later. A phone is reached when fire comes within 50 m of it within 3 hours of take-off. Warned in time: an alert at Level 3 or above delivered before the fire arrives, as a share of the phones reached. Lead time: how long before the fire arrives the first alert lands (median over phones, then over flights). Evacuated: told Level 4 or 5.
