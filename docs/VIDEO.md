# Demo video plan

The video runs 2:53, under the 3-minute limit. It goes on YouTube as a public video. Every flight in it is filmed from the real dashboard, using one of two methods. Most flights are rendered frame by frame from a live run of the simulator. The Mission tab is screen-recorded while it makes live calls. Every other shot is a real file from the repository, apart from the title and the end card. Nothing is mocked or staged. The raw takes are listed, with the commit and command of each, in `TAKES.md` beside them.

Every flight is a simulation and carries a label saying so. Evacuation alerts go to simulated phones only. Every number spoken or captioned is quoted exactly from a file in the repository (`results/`, `docs/`, `hardware/` or the README), and that file's name appears on screen in small grey type. The video is strictly civilian: wildfire scouting first, mountain search and rescue second.

There is no music, or only royalty-free music or music the entrant made. No third-party logos appear. NVIDIA and Nebius appear only as words, to describe the technology.

Facts about the cut:

* **Voiceover:** 407 words, about 141 words a minute. No shot is faster than 150 words a minute. Numbers are written as words, so the count is honest. Model and licence names are counted as written.
* **Valley footage:** 50 s of wildfire-valley footage is used, and 54 s is rendered.
* **Dashboard footage:** 91 s in all. This covers the rule that at least 1 minute shows the key modules in action.
* **Moments that respond to input:**
  * Row 8: an operator gives a zone the all-clear on a running valley flight.
  * Row 12: a gust front and a motor failure are injected into the running simulation.
  * Row 14: live calls to Nemotron on Nebius Token Factory.

## Recording

* **How the 3D shots are made.** `tools/record_shot.cjs` drives the running dashboard in headless Chromium through Playwright. It clicks only the controls a person would, or calls the same actions as the page's buttons. The 3D shots use `--film`, because a headless browser without a GPU draws the 3D view too slowly to screen-record smoothly. In film mode the page buffers the live run as it streams. The recorder then steps through it one video frame at a time, screenshotting each frame at 25 fps, and ffmpeg makes the .mp4. For a rule-supervised or replayed flight, the run is the same live run of the real simulator; only the filming changes.
* **What is simulated in the wildfire valley.** All of these come from the run:
  * the terrain and forest;
  * the fire's spread (Rothermel on the land cover, slope and wind), with burning and charred ground;
  * each fire's plume height;
  * the drones' smoke sightings and thermal mapping;
  * the station's located fires and forecast bands;
  * the zone alerts and the simulated phones they reach.

  The flame and smoke particles are drawn at the simulated burning cells and rise to the simulated plume tops. Only their look is decoration (see the comment at the top of `wildfire3d.js`). How long the ground and trees stay drawn as flaming, 300 s after the fire reaches them before they turn charred, is also a drawing choice.
* **What is drawn for looks elsewhere.** In the other scenarios, the scenery (hills, mountains, forests, shrubs, and the tree crowns above the simulated trunks) is drawn for looks, as are the hotspots' glow, embers and smoke (see `scene3d.js`). Drone positions and status, obstacles, camera coverage and everything in the panels come from the run. In every scenario the drones are drawn 4 times their real size so they show from 150 m away, and the legend says so.
* **Labels on screen.** Every flight carries a SIMULATION badge with the scenario, seed, playback speed and supervisor. Every time in seconds is simulated flight time.
  * Rows 1 and 11 are labelled "REPLAY OF A RECORDED NEMOTRON RUN", with "simulation".
  * Every valley shot, row 12 and the mission flight are labelled with the rule supervisor.
  * Row 8 is labelled "OPERATOR ACTION".
  * Only the Mission takes (row 14) are labelled "LIVE CALL"; the mission flight in E2 also carries "simulation".
* **Why rows 8 and 12 are not called live.** The all-clear and the injections are applied to the running simulation and then filmed frame by frame, so they are not called live. On a machine with a GPU, D can also be screen-recorded in real time with the buttons pressed by hand, and only that take is labelled "LIVE INPUT".
* **Editing.** Record each shot separately and cut in an editor. Edits are limited to cuts, crops, slow pushes, masks and overlaid labels; no frame of a flight is otherwise changed. The masks are the Mission tab's example-button row (its third button is "Convoy"), D's scenario description line and, on every rule-supervised shot, the header's Nemotron pill: it shows the server's mode, not the flight's supervisor.
* **Keeping the raw recordings with their commit.** `video/` is in `.gitignore`, so keep the raw takes there with a `video/TAKES.md` listing each file's name, command, server (A or B), `git rev-parse --short HEAD` and date. Takes C and D were filmed before the valley's last panel changes; those changes touch only the valley, and the forest and transit runs give the same numbers. Keep `video/live_cache.jsonl` and `evidence/dashboard_calls.jsonl` (also ignored) with the Mission takes as the record of the live calls.
