# Demo video plan

The video runs 2:56, under the 3-minute limit, and goes on YouTube as a public video. It follows the organisers' order: the problem in the first 12 seconds, then who it is for and the product working, with NVIDIA Nemotron and Nebius Token Factory named aloud by 0:35, and it closes on cost.

Every flight in it is filmed from the real dashboard, rendered frame by frame from a live run of the simulator in the cinema layout: the 3D view fills the frame, with the flight's title, clock, counts, crew briefs, alerts and notices on cards over it. The Mission takes were filmed the same way while making live calls. Every other shot is a real file from the repository, apart from the title, the end card and row 14's escape card, a plain table of figures quoted from `results/wildfire_initial_attack/REPORT.md` and its `PREREG.md`. Nothing is mocked or staged. The raw takes stay out of the repository; a list of them, with the commit and command of each, is kept beside them and archived with the uploaded video (see "Keeping the raw recordings with their commit" under Recording).

Every flight is a simulation and carries a label saying so. Evacuation alerts go to simulated phones only. Every number captioned is quoted exactly from a file in the repository (`results/`, `docs/`, `hardware/` or the README), and that file's name appears on screen in small grey type. A spoken number is the same figure, or rounded with "about" when the caption carries the exact one. The video is strictly civilian: finding wildfires now, with mountain search and rescue named as next; only a first search for a missing hiker in a simulated meadow is measured (`results/hiker_search/REPORT.md`). It never links any fire's deaths to how late it was found.

There is no music, or only royalty-free music or music the entrant made. No third-party logos or pictures appear. NVIDIA and Nebius appear only as words, to describe the technology.

Facts about the cut:

* **Voiceover:** 409 words with every `{{...}}` line in, about 139 words a minute; no row is faster than 149. Numbers are written as words, so the count is honest. Model and licence names are counted as written.
* **Valley footage:** about 80 s of wildfire-valley footage is used (rows 1-5, 7 and 8). The final valley takes were filmed once, on 21 October, after the features were frozen early, all under the pre-signed alert rules; the interim takes filmed on 20 October under today's rule are kept as spares.
* **Dashboard footage:** about 100 s in all, above the rule that at least 1 minute shows the key modules in action.
* **Moments that respond to input:**
  * Row 4: a live call to Nemotron on Nebius Token Factory compiles the operator's typed request.
  * Row 8: an operator gives a zone the all-clear on a running valley flight.
  * Row 11: a gust front and a motor failure are injected into the running simulation.
  * Row 12: live calls refused by Nemotron and by the keyword guard.

`{{...}}` marks a line or picture that waits on work still open (see "Waiting on" at the end). Until it lands, use the fallback given. Never caption or say a `{{...}}` figure before it is in the repository.

## Recording

* **How the 3D shots are made.** `tools/record_shot.cjs` drives the running dashboard in headless Chromium through Playwright. It clicks only the controls a person would, or calls the same actions as the page's buttons. The 3D shots use `--film`, because a headless browser without a GPU draws the 3D view too slowly to screen-record smoothly. In film mode the page buffers the live run as it streams; the recorder then steps through it one video frame at a time, screenshotting each frame at 25 fps, and ffmpeg makes the .mp4. For a rule-supervised or replayed flight, the run is the same live run of the real simulator; only the filming changes.
* **What is simulated in the wildfire valley.** All of these come from the run:
  * the terrain and forest;
  * the fire's spread (Rothermel on the land cover, slope and wind), with burning and charred ground;
  * each fire's plume height;
  * the drones' smoke sightings and thermal mapping;
  * the station's located fires, forecast bands and crew briefs (the head's bearing, cone and speed);
  * the zone alerts and the simulated phones they reach.

  The flame and smoke particles are drawn at the simulated burning cells and rise to the simulated plume tops; only their look is decoration (see the comment at the top of `wildfire3d.js`). How long the ground and trees stay drawn as flaming, 300 s after the fire reaches them before they turn charred, is also a drawing choice. The direction arrow is drawn straight from the brief's head now to its head in an hour.
* **What is drawn for looks elsewhere.** In the other scenarios, the scenery (hills, mountains, forests, shrubs, and the tree crowns above the simulated trunks) is drawn for looks, as are the hotspots' glow, embers and smoke (see `scene3d.js`). Drone positions and status, obstacles, camera coverage and everything on the cards come from the run. In every scenario the drones are drawn larger than their real size so they show from far away, and the title card says so.
* **Labels on screen.** The cinema title card carries "SIMULATION", the scenario, the swarm size, the supervisor and, in the valley, "alerts to simulated phones only"; the clock card carries the playback speed. Every time in seconds is simulated flight time.
  * Row 10's forest shot is labelled "REPLAY OF A RECORDED NEMOTRON RUN", with "simulation".
  * Every valley shot and row 11 are labelled with the rule supervisor.
  * Row 8's all-clear is labelled "OPERATOR ACTION".
  * Only the live-call takes (V0 in row 4, E3 in row 12) are labelled "LIVE CALL"; V0's take-off also carries "Simulation".
* **Why rows 8 and 11 are not called live.** The all-clear and the injections are applied to the running simulation and then filmed frame by frame, so they are not called live. On a machine with a GPU, D can also be screen-recorded in real time with the buttons pressed by hand, and only that take is labelled "LIVE INPUT".
* **Editing.** Record each shot separately and cut in an editor. Edits are limited to cuts, crops, slow pushes, masks and overlaid labels; no frame of a flight is otherwise changed.
* **Keeping the raw recordings with their commit.** `video/` is in `.gitignore`, so keep the raw takes there with a `video/TAKES.md` listing each file's name, command, server, `git rev-parse --short HEAD` and date. The final valley takes were filmed at one commit, 5c49de4, on 21 October. Keep the live-call cache, V0's call record (its `valley_compile_cache.jsonl` and `dashboard_calls.jsonl`, in `video/final/v0_call/`) and `evidence/dashboard_calls.jsonl` (all ignored) with the takes as the record of the live calls. E1b was filmed earlier the same day, at 4336954. Archive the folder with the uploaded video, so every shot can be traced to a run.
