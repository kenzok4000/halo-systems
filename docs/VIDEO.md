# Demo video plan

The video runs 2:55 (174.75 s), under the 3-minute limit, and goes on YouTube as a public video. It follows the organisers' order. The problem comes first: by about 0:10 the voice has said that lightning fires can smoulder unseen for hours and that one real Alberta fire went unseen until the next afternoon, and scene 1 ends at 0:14.4. Then come who it is for (a fire agency's team, 0:25.7) and the product working (scenes 2 to 10), with NVIDIA Nemotron and Nebius Token Factory named aloud in one sentence, from 0:30.0 to 0:35.5. It closes on cost (2:24.6) and open code (2:35.8).

Every flight in it is filmed from the real dashboard, rendered frame by frame from a live run of the simulator in the dashboard's clean film layout: the 3D view fills the frame, with one slim simulation badge, the clock and the labels the run produces. Scene 3 is a live call to Nemotron, filmed for the first cut. The five number cards and the end card are motion graphics drawn in a browser frame by frame, and every figure on them comes from a file in the repository. Nothing is mocked or staged. The raw takes and the edit's scripts stay out of the repository, in the ignored folder `video/`; a log of the takes, with the commit and command of each, is kept beside them and archived with the uploaded video (see "Keeping the raw recordings with their commit" under Recording).

Every flight is a simulation and carries a label saying so, and the end card says it in its largest line. Evacuation alerts go to simulated phones only. Every number in a caption, a card or the voice is the figure in a repository file (`results/`, `docs/`, `hardware/` or the README), or that figure rounded with "about" or "over" (350,135 hectares shown as "Over 350,000 ha", about 29 strikes as "about 30"), or a median said as "typically" or "half of those". Each number card names its source in small grey type; the On-screen text table gives the source of every caption and card line, and the table under it the source of every spoken number. The takes' own labels and V0's plan card carry the run's own figures, which are never quoted as results. The video is strictly civilian: finding wildfires, telling crews which way a fire will run and warning the people in its path, with mountain search and rescue named as next. It never links any fire's deaths to how late it was found.

There is no music: the only sound is the voice. No third-party logos or pictures appear. NVIDIA and Nebius appear only as words, to describe the technology.

**What changed in the remake, and why.** The first cut (2:56) squeezed 29 short voiceover lines, some of them fragments, into fixed picture windows, so the topic jumped every few seconds and the picture cut between unrelated places, and its 27 caption cards often showed a different sentence from the one being spoken. The remake aims to be easy to follow on a first viewing. The remake is voice first: one story in 13 scenes, each as long as its own sentences, so no sentence crosses a cut. Each filmed scene has one caption that says what the voice says, and animated number cards replace the chart stills. Six new valley takes were filmed in a cleaner film look, and in the edit their title panel drops "seed 4012" and "rule supervisor". The lab scenarios (the forest replay, the gust and motor failure, the refused requests, the search for failures), the fire-direction accuracy and the per-drone cost and break-even figures are left out; they stay in the files that hold them.

Facts about the cut:

* **Voiceover:** 391 words in 28 sentences, about 134 words a minute overall. Numbers are written as words, so the count is honest.
* **Narration:** a synthetic voice, because the entrant lost their voice. Kokoro-82M text-to-speech (Apache-2.0), run locally with the stock voice af_heart at speed 0.95, speaks the storyboard's sentences one by one, and the picture is laid to the voice. The end card says the video is narrated by a synthetic voice; on YouTube, Studio's "AI use" is set to Yes and the description says the same.
* **Timing:** each scene starts 0.45 s before its first sentence, its sentences are 0.38 s apart, and it holds 0.75 s after its last. Scenes 4 and 6 are held to 10 s and 9 s so their pictures can play out, scene 5 to 13 s, its take playing 12.58 s and then holding its last frame, so its caption, which comes in with "forward-looking", stays up about 4.5 s, and scene 13, the end card, to 19 s, so its last small lines stay up about 6 s before the picture fades to black over the last 0.8 s.
* **Valley footage:** about 72 s (scenes 2, 5 to 8 and 10), from six takes started on 27 October at one commit, 3297d9c.
* **Dashboard footage:** about 85 s with scene 3's typed request, above the rule that at least 1 minute shows the key modules in action.
* **Moment that responds to input:** scene 3, a live call to Nemotron on Nebius Token Factory that reads the operator's typed request. Nothing is injected and no operator acts in the valley takes.

Every figure the cut shows or speaks is already committed.

## On-screen text

Each filmed scene has one caption: a short line in large type with at most two small lines under it, on a fully opaque dark rounded panel at the lower left (so map labels behind it do not read through; scene 2's title panel is opaque too), clear of the take's own card (scene 3's sits at the top, scene 2's is a larger title, and scene 5's panel is narrower, to stay clear of Fire 3's label). It starts to fade in 0.2 s before its sentence starts, never sooner than 0.3 s into the scene, and has faded out 0.25 s before the scene ends (both fades take 0.3 s), so it says what the voice is saying. Scenes 5 and 10 are the two exceptions. Scene 5's caption names Fires 3 and 4, so it comes in with "forward-looking", as their labels land, rather than with the start of its sentence. Scene 10's comes in 1.9 s into the scene, once the take's Fire 1 label has cleared the panel's area, rather than 0.2 s before its sentence. The number cards are their scenes' captions: each line appears at the time given, set from the voice to the words that say it, and stays to the end of the scene. Chips and kickers are drawn in capitals, except the kickers of scenes 11 and 12, which are drawn as white headlines. Every time below is video time.

Over every valley take (scenes 2, 5 to 8 and 10) the badge at the upper left reads "SIMULATION Wildfire valley ◆ HALO SYSTEMS" over "16 drones · alerts go to simulated phones only · drones drawn up to 4× actual size". This is the page's own badge with "seed 4012" and "rule supervisor" taken out and "alerts to" made "alerts go to" in the edit (see Recording). The clock at the upper right shows sim time and the playback speed. The take's own labels (place names, a wind reading, fire labels such as "Fire 1 · from smoke ±18 m" and "Fire 1 head in 1 h") come from the run; their figures are the take's own and are never quoted as results.

## Shot list

Every remake take runs from the repository root with `NODE_PATH=$(npm root -g)`, against the dashboard with no key, `env -u NEBIUS_API_KEY halo serve --port 8765`, which makes no model calls; V0, reused from the first cut, was filmed on the live-call server (see its row). In film mode `--from` is in simulated seconds and `--speed` is the playback speed. Only the speeds 1, 2 and 5, the page's own speed buttons, are used.

Each remake take is filmed by `video/remake/film.sh NAME OPTIONS`. The script writes the start time, name, commit and options to `video/remake/FILM_LOG.txt` and then runs:

```
node tools/record_shot.cjs --url http://127.0.0.1:8765 --film --fps 25 --cinema --clean --scenario wildfire_valley \
  --config improved+rule --seed 4012 --n 16 --alert-rule presigned OPTIONS --out <scratch folder>/NAME.mp4
```

It then copies the take to `video/remake/NAME.mp4` and logs "done". Every take is 1920x1080 at 25 fps, rendered two at a time, at about 11 to 16 s a frame (from the start and done times in `FILM_LOG.txt`). All six were started at commit 3297d9c on 27 October, are logged "done", and frames from each were checked (see Notes).

## Recording

* **How the 3D shots are made.** `tools/record_shot.cjs` drives the running dashboard in headless Chromium through Playwright. It clicks only the controls a person would, or calls the same actions as the page's buttons. The 3D shots use `--film`, because a headless browser without a GPU draws the 3D view too slowly to screen-record smoothly. In film mode the page buffers the live run as it streams; the recorder then steps through it one video frame at a time, screenshotting each frame at 25 fps, and ffmpeg makes the .mp4. The run is the same live run of the real simulator; only the filming changes.
* **The clean film look.** `--clean` (with `--film --cinema`) opens the page with `?clean=1`, added at commit 3297d9c. It draws one slim badge with every simulation label, the clock with the playback speed, notices as lower-third toasts, and no cards unless `--clean-cards` names them. In the 3D view it adds a low warm morning sun, softer haze and shadows, a film-like colour grade, richer smoke, drones with position lights and short trails of where they just flew, clearer alert zones and head arrows, and larger labels that give way to more important ones. `--clean-cards alerts` shows only the evacuation alerts card, large (r7 and r10). `--clean-toasts clear` lets through only operator-action notices, and no operator acts in these flights, so the takes carry no toasts. `--ss` (supersampling) was not used. The crane view is one of three new scripted camera moves (crane, dolly and low). All of this is display only: nothing the simulator computes changes, and without `--clean` every frame is drawn as before.
* **What is simulated in the wildfire valley.** All of these come from the run:
  * the terrain and forest;
  * the fire's spread (Rothermel on the land cover, slope and wind), with burning and charred ground;
  * each fire's plume height;
  * the drones' positions, smoke sightings and thermal mapping;
  * the station's located fires (and each fix's error, printed on its label), forecast bands and crew briefs (the head's bearing, cone and speed);
  * the zone alerts and the simulated phones they reach.
* **What is drawn for looks.** The flame and smoke particles are drawn at the simulated burning cells and rise to the simulated plume tops; only their look is decoration (see the comment at the top of `wildfire3d.js`). How long the ground and trees stay drawn as flaming, 300 s after the fire reaches them before they turn charred, is also a drawing choice. The sun, haze, colour grade, shadows and the drones' position lights are decoration; the trails follow the drones' simulated positions. The drones are drawn up to 4 times their real size so they show from far away, and the badge says so. The direction arrow is drawn straight from the brief's head now to its head in an hour.
* **The title panel swap.** For first-time viewers, the edit swaps the valley takes' title panel for the same panel without "seed 4012" and "rule supervisor". `video/remake/motion/header/make_header.py` draws the panel from the clean layout's own styles (`src/halo_systems/dashboard/static/style.css`), reading "Wildfire valley" over "16 drones · alerts go to simulated phones only · drones drawn up to 4× actual size". In every frame of scenes 2, 5 to 8 and 10, `video/remake/make_remake.py` blurs the old panel inside its rounded outline, as the page's backdrop blur does, and lays the new text over it. The SIMULATION badge, the simulated-phones note and the drone-size note stay. The seed and the supervisor are in this document and in `video/remake/film.sh`, whose fixed flags every logged take adds.
* **Labels on screen.** Every valley frame carries the SIMULATION badge and the simulated-phones note; the clock carries the playback speed, and every time on it is simulated flight time. Scene 3's caption says the call is live and that the wait was shortened in filming (film mode cuts the wait for the compiler). The number cards about simulated valleys carry a "Simulation" chip; scene 1's card is marked "A real fire"; scene 4 tests reading typed requests, not a flight.
* **Number cards and the end card.** `video/remake/motion/cards.js` and `cards.css` define the card types used (timeline, bars, duo, stat and end). `video/remake/motion/render_motion.cjs` loads each card in headless Chromium, sets every animated element for each frame's time, screenshots it at 25 fps and makes an H.264 clip. `video/remake/gen_shots.py` writes `shots.json` from the voice's timeline, placing each timed line at the words that say it (by their share of the sentence's characters, 0.1 s early). The exceptions: scene 5's caption comes in 0.2 s before "forward-looking"; scene 10's comes in 1.9 s into the scene, once the take's Fire 1 label has cleared the panel's area; the sub lines of scenes 4, 9 and 11 come in 1.6 s, 0.4 s and 0.3 s after the times for "Hand-written", "Even against" and "In forty"; scene 11's source line comes in with the 37% rather than at the card's start; and the end card's lines come in as their sentences start, its two unspoken small lines 0.6 s and 0.9 s after "Every flight shown is a simulation." starts. Scene 1's timeline draws its track and progress bar only as its first point lands, so the card does not open on an empty bar. Scene 4's background is a blurred frame of V0, scenes 9 and 11 have blurred frames of r5 (sim about 40.2 s, with the rings) and r10 (sim about 223.4 s), and scene 12 shows Halo's assembled scout drone, `video/remake/motion/drone.png`, a crop of `hardware/renders/three_quarter.png`, with a caption under it. Scene 9's card adds a row of 20 small squares, one per valley, each with 5 orange dots for its fires, each dot in its own cell of a 3 x 3 grid so none touch, which appear one after another as the voice says "twenty valleys"; the cards of scenes 11 and 12 draw their kicker as a white headline instead of small orange capitals. Every value on a card is the published figure, or that figure rounded with "about" or "over", as the On-screen text table shows.
* **The voice.** `video/remake/make_voice2.py` speaks `video/remake/script.json` with Kokoro-82M (Apache-2.0) on the local CPU, stock voice af_heart, speed 0.95. Each sentence is spoken on its own, trimmed of silence and cached by voice, speed and spoken text, so an edit re-speaks only what changed. "Nemotron" and "Nebius" are given phonetic spellings everywhere, and a stressed "and" (scenes 3, 5, 6 and 9) and "than" (scene 11) keep those words clear. The script then lays the sentences out with the scene timing above, normalises the track to -16 LUFS (true peak -1.5 dB, high-pass at 70 Hz, 48 kHz stereo) and writes `voice.wav` and `timeline.json`.
* **Assembly.** `video/remake/make_remake.py` renders the number cards at the length each scene needs and draws each caption, a fully opaque panel, as an image with a transparent surround. It cuts each scene's picture to its scene length plus 0.4 s (the last scene to its own length; scenes 3 and 5 play their takes 8.9 s and 12.58 s and hold the last frame for the rest), and joins the scenes with 0.4 s dissolves that start at each scene's voice time, so the dissolve ends before the first sentence. It then lays the captions over with 0.3 s fades, each from 0.2 s before its sentence starts (never sooner than 0.3 s into the scene) or, for scenes 5 and 10, from its own time, fades the last 0.8 s to black and adds the voice. The output is H.264 (CRF 17) with AAC audio at 192 kb/s, 1920x1080 at 25 fps, plus `remake.srt` with one sentence per subtitle, from the sentence's start to 0.2 s after its end.
* **Editing.** Edits are limited to cuts, dissolves, scene 3's crop and held frame, scene 5's held last frame, overlaid captions, the title panel swap and the fade to black over the end card's last 0.8 s. No frame of a flight is otherwise changed.
* **Keeping the raw recordings with their commit.** `video/` is in `.gitignore`. Keep the remake's takes in `video/remake/` with `FILM_LOG.txt`, which lists for each take its start time, name, commit (`git rev-parse --short HEAD`), options and the time it was done; all six were started at 3297d9c on 27 October. Keep the edit's inputs and scripts beside them: `script.json`, `shots.json`, `gen_shots.py`, `make_voice2.py`, `build/voice/af_heart/` (with `voice.wav` and `timeline.json`), `make_remake.py`, `film.sh` and `motion/`, and the cut itself, `build/remake.mp4` with `build/remake.srt`. Keep also the first cut's card renderer, `video/cut/render_cards.cjs`, which `make_remake.py` and `motion/header/make_header.py` use to draw the captions and the title panel. V0 stays in `video/` with the first cut's `video/TAKES.md`, its call record (`valley_compile_cache.jsonl` and `dashboard_calls.jsonl`) in `video/final/v0_call/`, the live-call cache and `evidence/dashboard_calls.jsonl` (all ignored).
